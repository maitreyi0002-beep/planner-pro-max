# Decision table

Every row is a fork you take before writing. This is what makes the output a generated plan rather than a template with a name at the top.

Question numbers refer to `conversation.md`.

---

## Forks that change the whole document

| Signal | The plan becomes |
|---|---|
| **Q1 thin after two follow-ups** | Don't build it. Their gap is a body of work, not visibility. Offer the build-first version: one project, shipped publicly, before any publishing cadence. |
| **Q5 empty** | Weeks 1–3 are reading and teardowns, not publishing. Opinions need input first. Their take inventory gets written in week 4 from what they've read, not in week 1 from nothing. |
| **Q3 links show under ~500 followers** | Reply-engine-first: roughly 70% replies to other people, 30% original. They borrow audiences before building one. Original posting into an empty room is the most common way people conclude this doesn't work. |
| **Q3 links show over ~3,000** | Post-first. Distribution already exists; the gap is a reason to be followed. |
| **Q2 says internal promotion** | Mostly internal visibility — documents, demos, cross-team work, being the person who writes the thing everyone forwards. Public posting drops to a minor track. Do not hand someone a LinkedIn plan when their goal is a promotion in a company that doesn't read LinkedIn. |
| **Q2 says employed** | Halve the hours from whatever they claimed. Remove any take touching their employer's market. Build blocks move to weekends. |

## Forks that change parts

| Signal | The plan becomes |
|---|---|
| **Writing observed as effortful** | Teardowns, carousels, annotated screenshots, clips. No essay cadence. Captions under 100 words. Never prescribe long-form to someone whose answers were three words. |
| **Writing observed as comfortable** | Essays carry the plan. A weekly long-form piece becomes the spine and the short posts become distribution for it. |
| **Q8 shows low appetite for conflict** | Evidence posts and teardowns carry it. No hard takes — they won't publish them, and a plan full of unpublished posts is a failed plan. |
| **Q8 excludes daily posting** | Three higher-effort posts a week. Never prescribe a cadence they already refused. |
| **Q8 excludes video** | No YouTube, no talking-head clips. Screen recordings without a face are usually still acceptable — confirm rather than assume. |
| **Q10 stalled at week N before** | Make week N the lightest week in the plan, with the most visible win scheduled inside it. |
| **Q2 branch surfaced legal constraints** | Rewrite affected takes from first principles. Never suggest publishing employer IP, internal research, or unshipped work. |
| **Q3 shows a portfolio that's years stale** | Week 1 is updating it. No amount of reach helps when the thing people click is out of date. |
| **Q3 shows existing published work** | The project slate extends it. A half-built thing finished ships sooner and reads better than a new thing started. |
| **Q9 audience is in a different timezone** | Set the publishing slot for their audience's morning, not the person's convenience. Say explicitly why, or they'll drift back to posting when it suits them. |

## Combinations worth noticing

**Employed + low risk appetite + under 500 followers.** The most constrained case and more common than any other. Do not try to write an ambitious plan here. Three posts a week, all evidence-based, built on one project, with the reply engine doing the audience work. Say plainly that this is a 6-month arc, not 90 days.

**Rich Q1 + empty Q5.** They've done impressive work and have no opinions about it yet. This is the highest-upside case in the whole method — the receipts are there, the positions just haven't been articulated. Spend extra time in the interview mining question 5, and if it still comes up empty, the first three weeks are reading and the plan says so.

**Empty Q1 + strong Q5.** Lots of opinions, nothing shipped. Tempting, because they're fun to talk to. Refuse anyway: opinions without receipts read as noise, and publishing them at volume damages their reputation rather than building it.
